The 3 Sounds of "-ed"

Regular verbs in English always end in -ed in the past tense, but they are pronounced in 3 distinct ways based on the final sound of the base verb. Teach your students to feel their vocal cords!

Unvoiced Ending /t/ Sound

If the base verb ends in a voiceless air puff: p, k, s, ch, sh, f, x.

Your throat shouldn't vibrate on the last letter. The "-ed" turns into a sharp "T".

Voiced Ending /d/ Sound

If the base verb ends in a humming voice: l, n, r, g, v, s, z, b, m, or vowels.

Your vocal cords are already vibrating, so keep the vibration rolling into a smooth "D".

Extra Syllable /ɪd/ Sound

If the base verb already ends in a hard dental sound: t or d.

It's physically impossible to add another T or D directly, so you must create an extra syllable: "id".
